# FOMC Meeting Summary - January 2026

## Overview

The Federal Open Market Committee decided to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 5.25-5.50 percent, reflecting the Committee's assessment that inflation remains elevated and the labor market remains resilient.

## Key Observations

### Inflation Outlook

Committee members noted that inflation has shown modest progress toward the 2 percent objective over the past year, but remains above target. The Committee emphasized that it will need to see more consistent evidence of sustained inflation decline before considering adjustments to the policy stance.

### Labor Market

The labor market remains solid, with unemployment at 4.1 percent. Job gains have been moderate, and labor force participation has trended up. Wages have increased at an elevated pace but show signs of moderation.

### Economic Projections

Members generally expect GDP growth of 2.0-2.5 percent in 2026, with inflation trending down to around 2.5 percent by year-end. The median projection for the federal funds rate at the end of 2026 is 4.75 percent, implying two 25bp cuts from current levels.

## Forward Guidance

The Committee will continue to assess the evolving economic environment in determining the timing and extent of future policy adjustments. Decisions are dependent on incoming data and the assessment of the economic outlook.

Several members noted that downside risks to the labor market could warrant policy easing in the latter half of the year if employment were to soften materially.

## Market Implications

The decision to hold rates steady was largely expected by markets. However, the forward guidance suggesting potential cuts later in the year has supported risk assets and put some pressure on the dollar.

Treasury yields declined modestly on the announcement, with the 10-year yield falling 5bp to 4.35 percent. The 2-year yield remained anchored near 4.75 percent, reflecting expectations for a shallower path of rate cuts than previously anticipated.

The curve modestly steepened on the news, as markets priced out near-term cuts while maintaining expectations for later-year easing.
