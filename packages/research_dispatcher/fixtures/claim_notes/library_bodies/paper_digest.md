## Citation & link
- **Title:** Synthetic Cost Shocks and Producer Prices
- **Authors:** Ada Meridian (NYU, NBER), Blake Quill (Atlanta Fed), Casey Vale (Emory)
- **Series:** Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta Working Paper 2099-01
- **Page:** example.org/wp
## Summary
- **Question:** How much of a shipping-cost move reaches producer prices?
- **Data:** Confidential microdata (synthetic).
- **Method:** Local projections (synthetic).
## Key findings (paper's numbers)
- **Normal times: about zero.** OLS gamma is near zero and insignificant in the synthetic sample.
- **Crisis: an order of magnitude larger, and building.** IV crisis pass-through rises across horizons in the synthetic sample.
- **Aggregate (model-implied):** peak contribution to 12m PPI is **0.22pp** under the synthetic calibration.
- **Authors' bottom line:** "the nature of the shock matters as much as its size."
## Implications for inflation
- **Pass-through depends on the state, not on a fixed coefficient.** Narrow shocks do almost nothing; broad ones pass through.
