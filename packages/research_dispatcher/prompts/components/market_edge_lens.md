MARKET EDGE LENS
Use this as a meta-prompt while analyzing the corpus. Do not answer every question mechanically. Use it to decide what matters, what is fragile, and what deserves elevation into a through-line.

Ask:
1. Where is the consensus, and where does it break down?
   - Treat broad agreement as baseline context.
   - Prioritize disagreements between credible sources and identify whether the split comes from assumptions, data interpretation, horizon, sequencing, or mechanism.

2. Which macro variables are load-bearing, and what signposts would flip the view?
   - Surface the variables everyone is watching.
   - Identify the triggers, thresholds, or incoming evidence that would force a source to change its mind.

3. Which risks are being systematically underweighted or hand-waved away?
   - Elevate caveats that recur quietly across multiple reports.
   - Small caveats repeated across sources can matter more than a loudly stated base case.

4. Are there hidden contradictions inside an apparent consensus cluster?
   - Distinguish agreement on destination from disagreement on path, driver, or trade expression.
   - If sources all want the same headline trade for different structural reasons, capture that fracture.

5. What is the implied positioning embedded in each view?
   - Every forecast implies a trade, hedge, or flow preference.
   - Make the positioning explicit even when the report states it indirectly.

6. Which views are time-sensitive versus durable?
   - Separate tactical catalyst calls from structural regime views.
   - Do not merge short-horizon trades with multi-quarter or multi-year narratives unless the bridge is explicit.

7. Where do geopolitical, policy, and market-structure risks intersect with rates-specific views?
   - Look for through-lines that sit at the intersection of domains rather than inside a single silo.
   - Penalize incomplete rates views that ignore obvious fiscal, reserve, funding, issuance, or policy plumbing.

8. What second- and third-order effects are being traced out?
   - Prefer sources that move past the first-order rates reaction.
   - Elevate through-lines that connect rates views to FX, credit, equities, liquidity, or positioning spillovers when supported by the corpus.

9. Is there a coherent narrative about sequencing, or only a destination?
   - Direction without path is incomplete.
   - Prioritize reports that explain the order of events, catalysts, and transition risks.

10. What shared assumption do bulls and bears both need to be true, and how fragile is it?
   - The most important risk may be a load-bearing assumption embedded across opposing views.
   - Surface where the market could be offsides if that assumption breaks.

META-QUESTION
Ask throughout: what is the market being paid to believe right now, and which reports are pointing, even obliquely, toward a world where that belief is wrong?

OUTPUT IMPLICATIONS
- Prefer through-lines that expose fragile consensus, hidden disagreement, conditional flip points, underweighted tail risks, sequencing errors, or cross-domain spillovers.
- Treat plain consensus as necessary context, not sufficient edge.
- If a through-line cannot say why the market belief could be wrong, it is probably too shallow to elevate.
