"Accessible version # US Rates Watch # Backend fragility 26 January 2026 Corrected In Monitoring the likelihood of a late ‘22 UK dynamic in US rates we put recent episodes of relatively high fragility at the backend of the US curve (over ’24 and early ’25) in the context of the 2022 debt sustainability episode on the UK curve (colloquially referred to as the “Truss episode”). In this analysis, we measured fragility in terms of the dynamics of the 10s30s sovereign slope relative to the swaps curve slope, combined with the 30y swap spreads dynamic. Specifically, we take the product of {the frequency of moves where the 10s30s sov curve steepens significantly more than 10s30s swaps} and {the frequency of 30y swap spreads tightening during a selloff in 30y swap rates}. # Recent UK &#x26; US dynamic… When we update the results of this analysis for the recent dynamic of the UK and US backend, we note: - A relatively benign dynamic in UK rates recently with the average frequency of fragility moves over ’24, ’25, and ’26 ytd of c.7%, 11% and 13% respectively, and few peaks above 30% vs a ’22 average of 21% and a 100% peak over the “Truss episode” (Exhibit 1) … - … But higher averages over ’24 and ’25 for the US curve of 23% and ‘24%, respectively, with peaks in normalized frequencies of c.60-70%, but an average frequency of only 11% ytd &#x26; a clear decay in frequencies over late ’25 (Exhibit 2). These results highlight (1) a higher degree of chronic fragility at the backend of the US curve in recent years, vs the acute fragility episode in UK rates in ’22, but also (2) a more benign dynamic more recently likely driven by lower volatility, US Treasury efforts to balance supply and demand in Treasuries, and broader financial repression efforts (see US rates: rate vol &#x26; financial repression). # … Vs. recent fragility episode in JGBs We apply the same framework above to the JGBs curve. We note that: - The average fragility frequencies stayed in a relative benign regime even after the BoJ exited YCC at &#x3C; 5% levels (in ’23 and ’24) but increased to more normalized levels of c.10%+ more recently. - The recent fragility episode seems to be closer to the acute fragility seen in the “Truss episode” (both in terms of the type of move and the catalysts for the move), but with a normalized frequency of only c.45% which did not push the BoJ to act as a buyer of last result (as was the case in the “Truss episode”). However, this is not the only 30%+ peak in the recent JGB dynamic, with another c.50% peak in early ’25 suggesting a potential increase in chronic fragility at the backend of the JGB curve.
